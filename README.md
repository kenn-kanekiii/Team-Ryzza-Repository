# Team-Ryzza-Repository

Coastal and Marine Biodiversity Sightings Citizen Science Log
Supports SDG 14 – Life Below Water

Project Description

The Coastal and Marine Biodiversity Sightings Citizen Science Log is a system made for local researchers and coastal communities. 
It lets them log, track, and analyze ecological data such as species sightings, coral health, and coastal debris. 
The project ties directly into a global sustainable development goal, SDG 14 (Life Below Water).

Problem Being Addressed

Ocean and coastal ecosystems are under pressure from coral bleaching, pollution, and unsustainable practices, but the data about them is usually scattered or not recorded at all. 
Local communities see these changes first, yet they don't have a simple way to report what they observe, and researchers don't have a central place to collect and filter that information. 
This project gives both groups one system to record observations and get warned early when something serious is happening.

Project Objectives

Provide a simple way for users to log species sightings, coral health, and coastal debris.
Automatically alert users and researchers when a high-severity entry is logged.
Help researchers filter their data and export monthly reports in the format they prefer.
Warn users about seasonal events and risky sea conditions in their area.
Support SDG 14 by making marine conservation data easier to collect and use.

Target Users

Local researchers and marine scientists
Coastal communities (fisherfolk, residents, local organizations)
Citizen scientists and volunteers who monitor the coast

Main Features

Ecological data logging – record species sightings, coral health, and coastal debris.
Automatic alert handler – triggers when a high-severity entry is logged, for example a critical coral bleaching percentage (e.g., above 70%) or a hazardous debris concentration.
Flexible report export – researchers can choose how they want to export their filtered monthly report.
Seasonal alerts – warns users about spawning seasons, turtle nesting periods, and fishing bans in their area.
Weather and sea-condition warnings – flags rough seas, heat waves, or runoff that may stress reefs (for example, heat waves can cause coral bleaching).
